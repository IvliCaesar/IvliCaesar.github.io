01 Overview

Overview

The center of gravity of this profile is doctoral-level research in pure mathematics. The doctoral dissertation, Sobre la convergencia de la sucesión espectral ortogonal (UNAM, 2022), and the resulting joint paper with Pablo Peláez establish strong convergence of the orthogonal spectral sequence in Voevodsky’s triangulated category of motives — work that has since been built on and cited by several papers in algebraic cycle theory. This sits inside a broader research program spanning algebraic geometry, stable and motivic homotopy theory, and topos–model theory (joint with Eduardo Duñez and José Iovino), with earlier graduate work in the Langlands program (master’s thesis on Drinfeld’s reciprocity law) and in random matrix theory and L-functions (undergraduate thesis).

Alongside this research core, the profile includes applied quantitative work in econometrics and mathematical finance, and a teaching practice in applied mathematics, statistics, and probability that supports the institutional mission of research-driven education.

Topos Theory & Model Theory

A geometric theory T determines a classifying topos Set[T] that classifies its models: for any topos E, models of T in E correspond to geometric morphisms E → Set[T]. Kripke–Joyal semantics supplies the internal, sheaf-theoretic notion of forcing that makes this correspondence precise sheaf by sheaf, and the Diaconescu–Deligne–Barr circle of theorems characterizes which Grothendieck toposes arise this way and how their points and internal geometric logic determine them up to the relevant equivalence (joint with Eduardo Dueñez and José Iovino). A second, ongoing line with the same collaborators extends Birkhoff–von Neumann quantum logic — classically valued in the lattice of closed subspaces of a Hilbert space — to a Sòler–valued model theory, using Sòler’s theorem characterizing Hilbert spaces among orthomodular spaces to control which lattices admit a compatible model-theoretic semantics. Independent solo results include a non-existence theorem for a deciding meet-filter on the modular ortholattice MO₂ — ruling out a natural strategy for extending classical two-valued semantics to this quantum setting — and a homological-projector statement obtained via persistent homology, linking topological invariants of a filtration to spectral projectors in the underlying lattice.

  • Model theory of Grothendieck toposes: classifying toposes, Kripke–Joyal semantics, and the Diaconescu–Deligne–Barr theorems (joint with Eduardo Dueñez and José Iovino)
  • Sòler–valued model theory extending Birkhoff–von Neumann quantum logic (joint, in progress)
  • Solo original results: non-existence of a deciding meet-filter on MO₂, and a homological-projector statement via persistent homology

Urban Welfare & Mobility

Joint work with Akito Kamei examining how housing affordability and commuting burden shape urban welfare in Mexico City, using the spatial structure of mobility from INEGI’s 2017 Origin–Destination Survey to study transit-network optimization and ridership flows. The paper builds the descriptive and spatial groundwork — mode choice, commuting burden, transit access, persistent‑homology analysis of the network’s most robust cycles — that a Rosen–Roback hedonic rent regression would need as inputs, under a formal spatial‑equilibrium definition of amenity capitalization into rents and wages.

  • Housing affordability, commuting burden, and urban welfare in Mexico City (with Akito Kamei)
  • Spatial structure of mobility from the 2017 Origin–Destination Survey (INEGI)
  • Transit-network optimization and persistent‑homology analysis of ridership flows
  • Rosen–Roback hedonic welfare framework: spatial equilibrium and amenity capitalization into rents and wages
  • Work in progress: estimating the hedonic rent regression once rent/housing-price, crime, and amenity–GIS data are incorporated (candidates under evaluation: 2020 Census housing module, INEGI Encuesta Intercensal 2025, SESNSP crime incidence series)

Wavelet Theory ↗ Lecture Notes

Self-contained lecture notes developing wavelet theory from first principles, from L² Hilbert-space foundations through multiresolution analysis, the Daubechies and Cohen–Daubechies–Feauveau wavelet families, to nonlinear approximation theory and its applications.

  • L²(ℝ) theory: Riesz–Fischer, the projection theorem, and the Duffin–Schaeffer frame theorem
  • Multiresolution analysis: the two-scale relation, the QMF condition, and existence/uniqueness of the mother wavelet
  • Daubechies D₂ₙ wavelets via the Bézout equation; the symmetry obstruction motivating biorthogonal (CDF) wavelets
  • Continuous and discrete wavelet transforms: the Calderón–Grossmann–Morlet theorem and Sweldens’s lifting factorisation
  • Besov/Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, Donoho–Johnstone shrinkage, and applications to JPEG 2000, compressed sensing, and turbulence analysis

RGB–LED Sensor Fusion ↗ Paper

Joint work with Oscar Amador and J. Salazar Hernández on data-driven spectral reflectance reconstruction from RGB cameras augmented with a small number of LED channels — an ill-posed inverse problem made tractable, and its LED wavelengths chosen, by design rather than by trial and error.

  • PCA latent basis with data-driven dimension selection for the reflectance spectrum
  • D-optimal LED wavelength selection maximizing the log-determinant of the Fisher information matrix
  • Bayesian reconstruction with closed-form posterior uncertainty quantification
  • Sensing-quality diagnostics — condition number and log-determinant — independent of the estimator or test sample
  • On a controlled benchmark, 4 LED channels cut the condition number from 2.687 to 1.251 and reduce colorimetric error (ΔE₀₀: 1.72 vs. 2.31 for RGB‑only, p < 0.01)
