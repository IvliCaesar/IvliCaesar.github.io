01 Overview

Overview

The center of gravity of this profile is doctoral-level research in pure mathematics. The doctoral dissertation, Sobre la convergencia de la sucesión espectral ortogonal (UNAM, 2022), and the resulting joint paper with Pablo Peláez establish strong convergence of the orthogonal spectral sequence in Voevodsky’s triangulated category of motives — work that has since been built on and cited by several papers in algebraic cycle theory. This sits inside a broader research program spanning algebraic geometry, stable and motivic homotopy theory, and topos–model theory (joint with Eduardo Duñez and José Iovino), with earlier graduate work in the Langlands program (master’s thesis on Drinfeld’s reciprocity law) and in random matrix theory and L-functions (undergraduate thesis).

Alongside this research core, the profile includes applied quantitative work in econometrics and mathematical finance, and a teaching practice in applied mathematics, statistics, and probability that supports the institutional mission of research-driven education.

Teachers and mentors: Alberto Verjovsky, Eduardo Dueñez, Timothy Gendron, Pablo Peláez Menaldo (Chebyshev Laboratory, St. Petersburg State University), Víctor Manuel Pérez–Abreu Carrión (CIMAT), Adrián Zenteno, José Iovino, Miguel Ángel García Álvarez, and Enrique Hernández–Lemus (Computational Systems Biology and Integrative Genomics Lab, Instituto Nacional de Medicina Genómica).

Scholarly discussions: a set of personal notes from Dustin Clausen (IHES), following up on discussions during his February 2026 IHES lecture series, on the interaction between algebraic K-theory and chromatic homotopy theory — stable ∞-categories and spectra, the redshift phenomenon (Rognes; Ausoni–Rognes) by which algebraic K-theory raises chromatic height, and the recent disproof of the telescope conjecture (Burklund–Hahn–Levy–Schlank) — together with two addenda, on shtukas and on condensed mathematics, written at my request.

Algebraic Geometry ↗ Thesis

An ongoing program, joint with Pablo Peláez, develops the consequences of the Galindo–Peláez convergence theorem for Peláez’s birational-cover filtration on Chow groups: an explicit basis and dimension formula for the Griffiths group of a product of non-isogenous elliptic curves, isogeny invariance, a test on the Ceresa cycle, pullback/transfer/Gysin compatibility from a single naturality lemma, base change and Galois descent along finite extensions, an explicit curve where the filtration provably disagrees with the classical Bloch–Ogus coniveau filtration despite equal length, and comparisons with Voevodsky’s slice filtration and Bondarko’s weight structure.

  • Higher Chow groups and motivic cohomology
  • Bloch–Beilinson filtrations
  • Abelian varieties and Chow correspondences

Topos Theory & Model Theory ↗ Thesis

A geometric theory T determines a classifying topos Set[T] that classifies its models: for any topos E, models of T in E correspond to geometric morphisms E → Set[T]. Kripke–Joyal semantics supplies the internal, sheaf-theoretic notion of forcing that makes this correspondence precise sheaf by sheaf, and the Diaconescu–Deligne–Barr circle of theorems characterizes which Grothendieck toposes arise this way and how their points and internal geometric logic determine them up to the relevant equivalence (joint with Eduardo Dueñez and José Iovino). A second, ongoing line with the same collaborators extends Birkhoff–von Neumann quantum logic — classically valued in the lattice of closed subspaces of a Hilbert space — to a Sòler–valued model theory, using Sòler’s theorem characterizing Hilbert spaces among orthomodular spaces to control which lattices admit a compatible model-theoretic semantics. A further, in-progress direction, joint with Eduardo Dueñez and José Iovino, asks how far this Sòler-valued semantics can be pushed computationally — toward idempotents, deep computations, and homological readings of orthomodular lattices, connecting to solenoids and to social choice.

  • Model theory of Grothendieck toposes: classifying toposes, Kripke–Joyal semantics, and the Diaconescu–Deligne–Barr theorems (joint with Eduardo Dueñez and José Iovino)
  • Sòler–valued model theory extending Birkhoff–von Neumann quantum logic (joint, in progress)
  • Ultrafilters, idempotents, and tameness: deep computations, orthomodular lattices, solenoids, and social choice (in progress, joint with Eduardo Dueñez and José Iovino)

Random Matrix Theory in Quantitative Finance

Joint work with Martín Rodolfo García Barrera, using the statistical-mechanical machinery of large random matrices—the Coulomb-gas/eigenvalue-repulsion picture behind the Marčenko–Pastur law and the Baik–Ben Arous–Péché spiked-covariance transition, Tracy–Widom extreme-value statistics of the spectral edge, and the free-probabilistic deconvolution behind optimal nonlinear shrinkage—to separate signal from noise in a 38-asset, 14.5-year covariance matrix of the Mexican Stock Exchange (BMV).

  • Spectral identification of exactly two economically interpretable factors (market, sector), confirmed against independent macroeconomic regressions and, via Tracy–Widom fluctuation theory, separated from the noise floor at better than 99% confidence
  • Analytic nonlinear shrinkage (Ledoit–Wolf) dominates linear shrinkage, eigenvalue clipping, and the raw sample estimator out-of-sample, on both a single window and a rolling reconstruction
  • Robust to a dynamic DCC–GARCH benchmark and to the heavy-tailed (non-Gaussian) returns actually present in the data
  • Extends beyond the minimum-variance portfolio to a CAPM-anchored efficient frontier and a Black–Litterman equilibrium posterior
  • Connects, via Dyson Brownian motion, to a proposed continuous-time bridge between static spectral cleaning and dynamic correlation models

Number Theory ↗ Morfismos ↗ Extended Notes

Master’s thesis, La ley de reciprocidad de Drinfeld (advisor: Timothy Mooney Gendron Thornton, UNAM, 2016), published in Morfismos (Cinvestav) Vol. 20, No. 2, pp. 1–66. Presents Drinfeld’s proof of the Langlands correspondence for GL₂ over function fields via Drinfeld modules, rigid cohomology, and Eichler–Shimura theory. The extended notes reorganize Drinfeld’s original article into a continuous narrative, from formal O-modules to the cohomological comparison producing automorphic and Galois realizations on the same space.

  • Langlands correspondence for GL₂ over function fields
  • Formal O-modules and Drinfeld modules (definition over fields, morphisms, rank, Drinfeld modules over schemes)
  • The Carlitz module and explicit class field theory; torsion and the finite-level reciprocity law
  • Torsion subgroup schemes, level structures, and representability of the fine moduli problem; compactification and cusps in rank two
  • A-lattices, analytic uniformization, and the converse uniformization theorem
  • Elliptic sheaves: Riemann–Roch, and the equivalence with Drinfeld modules
  • The Drinfeld upper half-plane, the Bruhat–Tits tree, and Čech cohomology of the upper half-plane (harmonicity, the special representation)
  • Adelic uniformization of the modular curve

RGB–LED Sensor Fusion

Joint work with Óscar Amador on data-driven spectral reflectance reconstruction from RGB cameras augmented with a small number of LED channels — an ill-posed inverse problem made tractable, and its LED wavelengths chosen, by design rather than by trial and error. The framework poses channel selection as a D-optimal experimental design problem: maximizing the determinant of the Fisher information matrix induced by a PCA-learned latent basis for the reflectance spectrum, with sensing-quality diagnostics (condition number, log-determinant) independent of the estimator or test sample, and a closed-form Bayesian posterior for the reconstruction itself.

  • PCA latent basis with data-driven dimension selection for the reflectance spectrum
  • D-optimal LED wavelength selection maximizing the log-determinant of the Fisher information matrix
  • Four structural results underpinning the design criterion: a rank-monotonicity proposition, an exact characterization of when it degenerates, an AM–GM inequality linking D- and A-optimality, and its identification with maximum expected Shannon information gain in the weak-prior limit — confirmed in practice far outside that asymptotic regime
  • A first-order perturbation bound (Weyl’s inequality) converting hardware calibration drift into an explicit design tolerance, plus a Cramér–Rao lower bound and a closed-form Bayesian posterior independently verified against Metropolis–Hastings MCMC
  • On a controlled benchmark, D-optimal design cuts the sensing operator’s condition number from 3.626 to 1.369 and colorimetric error by a factor of six under heteroscedastic noise; a leave-one-out validation on real, spectrophotometrically measured reflectance (the BabelColor ColorChecker dataset) confirms a five-to-sevenfold error reduction on measured, not synthetic, data
  • Structured neural estimators (a from-scratch transformer and 1D CNN) beat the D-optimal Bayesian pipeline on a majority of families/metrics given enough capacity, while unstructured baselines (MLP, autoencoder) never do
  • The design criterion extends beyond optics: the same Poisson and negative-binomial Fisher information matrices, derived and Monte-Carlo-verified, transfer the D-optimal criterion unchanged to probe/biomarker panel design in computational genomics
